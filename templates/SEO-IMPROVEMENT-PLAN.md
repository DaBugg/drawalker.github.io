# Phase 5 — SEO and Answer-Engine Production Readiness

## Purpose

Phase 5 converts each conceptual landing page into an evidence-led search plan for a real business.

It does **not** make the fictional concepts indexable. The existing industry sites must remain `noindex, nofollow` until their company identity, services, locations, people, claims, media, policies, and conversion systems are replaced and verified.

The objective is to make every future production site:

- discoverable for the right commercial or patient intent;
- clear enough to answer common questions without ambiguity;
- supported by verifiable first-party expertise and proof;
- technically crawlable and indexable only after approval;
- measurable through search, conversion, and user-experience data.

## Phase 5 outcome

Each adapted client site should leave this phase with:

1. A verified business fact sheet.
2. A search-intent and page map.
3. Approved page briefs and on-page metadata.
4. A first-party proof and media plan.
5. Valid structured data that matches visible content.
6. A technical indexing and measurement setup.
7. A signed launch gate before `noindex` is removed.

---

# 5A — Verify the real entity before keyword work

## Why this comes first

The concepts currently contain fictional companies, locations, people, capabilities, ratings, projects, and metrics. Keyword research performed against fictional facts would produce the wrong site architecture and could encourage unsupported claims.

Complete one source-of-truth fact sheet per real client.

## Required business facts

| Category | Required information |
|---|---|
| Identity | Legal name, public brand name, founding year, ownership description |
| Contact | Verified phone, monitored email, physical or service-area address |
| Market | Exact service area, excluded areas, remote or national availability |
| Offers | Services or products actually sold, primary offer, commercial priority |
| Audience | Buyer, patient, homeowner, investor, or user roles served |
| Proof | Verified projects, outcomes, reviews, credentials, licenses, awards |
| People | Real leaders, clinicians, authors, subject-matter reviewers, credentials |
| Process | Actual intake, quote, appointment, bid, demo, or engagement workflow |
| Policies | Privacy, terms, accessibility, warranty, finance, healthcare, or investor disclosures |
| Systems | CRM, scheduler, secure upload, analytics, consent, call tracking, hosting |

## Evidence rule

Every production claim must have an owner and source. Use one of these statuses:

- **Verified:** Approved source exists and can be cited internally.
- **Needs review:** Plausible but not yet approved.
- **Do not publish:** Fictional, outdated, confidential, or unsupported.

Do not convert illustrative metrics, ratings, availability, credentials, service areas, project outcomes, or software capabilities into production copy without verification.

# 5B — Search-intent and page mapping

## Mapping principle

Assign one dominant intent to each indexable page. Avoid creating many near-duplicate pages that differ only by a keyword or city name.

The homepage should own the clearest brand-plus-category proposition. Dedicated pages should exist only when the business can provide distinct value, proof, and decision support for that topic.

## Recommended concept maps

### Forgeworks Industrial

**Primary intent:** Source a qualified fabricator or manufacturer and submit a usable RFQ.

**Homepage theme:** Industrial fabrication and project supply for the verified service region.

**Recommended production pages:**

1. `/capabilities/` — overview of real manufacturing processes.
2. `/capabilities/cnc-machining/` — machines, materials, part envelope, tolerances, inspection, examples.
3. `/capabilities/welded-fabrication/` — processes, weld procedures, materials, assembly scale, examples.
4. `/capabilities/process-piping/` — applicable codes, materials, testing, documentation, delivery.
5. `/materials/` — supported materials, forms, finishes, sourcing constraints.
6. `/quality/` — inspection system, traceability, documentation, verified certifications.

8. `/projects/{project}/` — verified problem, scope, constraints, process, result.

# 5C — Page brief and on-page standard

Create a brief before writing each new page.

## Required page brief

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Page purpose | One sentence describing the visitor decision this page supports |
| Primary intent | Informational, commercial evaluation, local, transactional, or navigational |
| Primary audience | Specific role or patient/homeowner context |
| Main question | The most important question answered on the page |
| Primary action | One conversion or next step |
| Evidence | First-party proof required to support the page |
| Unique value | What this page provides beyond a generic category summary |
| Related pages | Parent, child, proof, location, and conversion links |
| Owner | Person accountable for accuracy |
| Reviewer | Subject-matter, clinical, legal, or compliance reviewer when needed |
| Review date | Date content was last substantively verified |

## On-page requirements

- One descriptive title and one H1 aligned to the page's actual purpose.
- A concise opening answer that identifies the service, audience, location or context, and next step.
- Logical H2 and H3 sections based on the decision journey—not a fixed SEO template.
- Real examples, constraints, process details, and evidence.
- Descriptive internal links rather than repeated “learn more” anchors.
- A visible author or reviewer where expertise is expected.
- A reviewed date for clinical, technical, financial, or operational guidance.
- Useful image alt text; decorative media should not carry essential text.
- A unique meta description written for accurate expectation-setting, not keyword repetition.
- A canonical URL using the final production origin.

## Answer-engine format

Answer-engine readiness is not a separate layer of keyword-stuffed copy. It comes from making important facts explicit, attributable, and easy to extract.

Use these patterns where they improve the visitor experience:

- a direct 40–80 word answer below a genuine question heading;
- concise definition, requirement, comparison, or process tables;
- numbered steps for sequential tasks;
- labeled constraints, exclusions, eligibility, and service areas;
- named authors and reviewers linked to biography pages;
- citations to primary sources for clinical, regulatory, technical, or market claims;
- visible dates for time-sensitive information;
- consistent organization, service, people, and location facts across the site.

Do not write repetitive FAQ blocks merely to target variations of the same phrase.

# 5D — Structured data plan

Structured data must describe content that visitors can see. It must never be used to turn fictional or hidden information into apparent proof.

## Recommended baseline

| Site type | Production schema candidates |
|---|---|
| All real organizations | `Organization`, `WebSite`, `WebPage`, `BreadcrumbList` |
| Local home service | Most specific valid `LocalBusiness` subtypes, real address/service facts |
| Clinic | `MedicalClinic`, real `Physician` or provider entities where appropriate |
| Software | `SoftwareApplication` when visible product facts support it |
| Articles/research | `Article` with real author, publisher, dates, headline, image |
| Project or service pages | Appropriate `Service`, `Product`, or other descriptive schema only when accurate |

## FAQ caution

Plain-language FAQs remain useful for visitors and machine understanding. Do not promise an FAQ rich result. Search features can be limited by eligibility, site type, query, and search-engine policy even when markup is valid.

## Validation

1. Validate JSON-LD syntax.
2. Run eligible types through Google's Rich Results Test.
3. Use Schema.org's validator for broader vocabulary checks.
4. Confirm every marked-up claim is visible and accurate.
5. Re-test the deployed URL through Search Console URL Inspection.
6. Monitor enhancement reports after release.

# 5E — Technical SEO and launch configuration

## Prelaunch technical checklist

- [ ] Final HTTPS production origin confirmed.
- [ ] One canonical URL per indexable page.
- [ ] Internal links use final crawlable URLs.
- [ ] Navigation and essential content work without interaction-only discovery.
- [ ] Titles, descriptions, H1s, and Open Graph data are unique and approved.
- [ ] Bespoke social images use verified brand and content.
- [ ] Structured data validates and matches visible content.
- [ ] `404` returns an actual HTTP 404 status in production.
- [ ] Redirect map covers every replaced or moved URL.
- [ ] XML sitemap contains canonical, indexable URLs only.
- [ ] `robots.txt` references the production sitemap and does not block required resources.
- [ ] Privacy, terms, consent, and accessibility information are approved.
- [ ] Forms use secure production endpoints with spam protection and error monitoring.
- [ ] Analytics and conversion events are documented and tested.
- [ ] Google Search Console is verified for the production property.
- [ ] Google Business Profile is claimed and consistent for eligible local businesses.
- [ ] Core Web Vitals are measured on representative mobile pages.
- [ ] Real images have dimensions, responsive sources, modern formats, and useful alt text.
- [ ] Video has a poster, preload strategy, captions when needed, and reduced-motion handling.

## Core Web Vitals targets

Use current field-data thresholds as the initial quality target:

- **LCP:** 2.5 seconds or less.
- **INP:** under 200 milliseconds.
- **CLS:** below 0.1.

These are experience targets, not a substitute for useful content, accurate entity information, or appropriate search intent.

## Indexing launch gate

Do not remove `noindex` until every item below has an owner and approval:

- [ ] Fictional identity and proof replaced.
- [ ] Search-intent map approved.
- [ ] Required production pages complete.
- [ ] Legal, clinical, financial, licensing, and compliance review complete where applicable.
- [ ] Secure conversion workflow tested.
- [ ] Canonical origin, redirects, sitemap, and robots configuration validated.
- [ ] Structured data and social previews validated.
- [ ] Analytics, Search Console, and conversion events verified.
- [ ] Final crawl confirms no fictional content or broken paths.
- [ ] Named launch approver authorizes the change from `noindex` to indexable.

Remove `noindex` in a controlled production release. Do not rely on `robots.txt` alone to prevent indexing; crawlers need access to read a page-level `noindex` rule.

# 5F — Measurement and improvement cycle

## Baseline events

Define one primary conversion per site and a small set of diagnostic events.

| Concept | Primary conversion | Useful diagnostic events |
|---|---|---|
| Forgeworks | Valid RFQ submitted | Capability selected, requirements viewed, upload started |
| Apexline | Bid invitation submitted | Market viewed, lifecycle used, project opened |
| Harborline | Overview request submitted | Project opened, map used, research viewed |
| Meridian | Working session requested | Framework used, engagement expanded, insight opened |
| RapidRoot | Appointment requested | Service selected, ZIP checked, call initiated |
| Palm Coast | Evaluation requested | Search used, condition selected, provider/location viewed |
| FleetAxis | Freight quote submitted | Route built, mode viewed, requirements viewed |
| SitePilot | Product demo requested | Role changed, workflow stage used, security viewed |
| Gallery | Concept opened | Filter used, view changed, “use this direction” selected |

## Reporting cadence

### First 30 days

- Confirm indexing and canonical selection.
- Resolve crawl, sitemap, structured-data, or redirect errors.
- Confirm conversion events and form delivery.
- Record branded versus non-branded query baseline.

### Days 31–90

- Compare impressions, clicks, click-through rate, landing-page engagement, and qualified conversions by intent.
- Improve pages with impressions but weak snippets or mismatched intent.
- Add missing proof and clearer answers before adding more pages.
- Review local profile actions for eligible local businesses.

### Quarterly

- Revalidate facts, authors, reviewers, dates, service areas, policies, and schema.
- Refresh pages only when the underlying information meaningfully changes.
- Merge, redirect, or remove pages that duplicate intent or provide insufficient value.
- Use real customer, sales, patient-access, and support questions to prioritize the next content brief.

## Success criteria

Phase 5 succeeds when:

- qualified organic visitors land on the page that best matches their decision;
- indexable pages contain verified, differentiated first-party information;
- business, people, service, and location facts are consistent;
- technical crawling and indexing are predictable;
- structured data is accurate and validated;
- local or high-trust pages show clear real-world authority;
- conversions can be attributed without collecting unnecessary data;
- content improvement is based on evidence rather than page-count growth.
